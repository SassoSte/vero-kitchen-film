# Lessons to retain

This is a concise record of observed project lessons, not permission to resume production.

1. **One design state across every camera.** The front/master images retained a top-freezer after the Bosch change. An appliance change includes doors, handles, height and the cupboard above—not just a brand name. Every dependent frame must be invalidated when the design changes.
2. **Current user reference is not an approved generated frame.** REF-K defines desired composition but contains an unwanted single-door fridge. Internal “visual pass,” user approval, exact manufacturer fidelity and installation feasibility are separate claims.
3. **Wall identity beats compass shorthand.** Wine/coffee belongs on the blank wall beside main fridge; spice/tech belongs on the other designated sink-side wall near microwave. Do not combine them into one tower because a prompt or plan orientation makes that convenient.
4. **Flush means flush with the wall face.** Projecting cabinets and above-DW coffee housings were rejected. Keep counters usable. Do not quietly replace a flush request with a box; also do not claim the required cavity is real without evidence.
5. **Use all procured sources before asking for more.** Original apartment-facing photos and four architectural SVGs already existed in `vero/deco`. The film repo initially held a subset, and REF-C/D were duplicates. Use the source register and hashes rather than treating labels or a stale README as evidence.
6. **Dimensions have evidence classes.** Module standards, photo estimates and marketing-render scaling differ. Record discrepancies (cap length, area, conversion, mirrored schematic) instead of enlarging the room or turning estimates into surveyed values.
7. **Compact closed size does not establish usable fit.** Check installed cutout, hinge-side wall, opening angle, handles, drawer/rack access and service clearances. French doors change both outer hinge relationships. A generated open door is not a clearance test.
8. **Source-image defects propagate into video.** The first quartz starting image invented a cabinet. The subsequent pilot still released a slab without seating it correctly. Fix geometry and the required end state before spending on motion retries.
9. **Review what happened, not what was prompted.** Stool pilot failed by facing away from the bar; “shrinking” was an initial misread and was withdrawn. Preserve corrections in the evidence record. Six-fps samples are not every-frame or full-playback acceptance.
10. **Atomic actions and cumulative state matter.** Thirteen editorial shots require many source takes. Floors, cabinets, appliances, lighting and staging must arrive in sequence and remain installed. Finished-view plates cannot serve every construction stage.
11. **Scope edits narrowly and inspect collateral changes.** Revisions introduced extra knobs, wrong fridge doors, ceiling-height cabinets, changed ceiling lights and a duplicated coffee station. Preserve originals and rejected variants; do not label a render accepted on appearance alone.
12. **Budget from job receipts, not shared-wallet movement.** Per-job quotes total 198.25; unrelated account balance changes occurred. A quote, available wallet or suggested cap increase is not spend authorization. Check pending jobs before retrying.
13. **Keep the active brief clean.** Accumulated historical appendices and stale state fields reintroduced rejected decisions. Active design belongs in the brief/state; old proposals belong in explicitly historical records. A higher filename version does not make it current.
14. **Stop before volume when a representative action fails.** Quartz and stool were rejected; fridge remained provisional. No full production batch should be based on those partial results. Cap repeated failures and change the source/end-state strategy.
